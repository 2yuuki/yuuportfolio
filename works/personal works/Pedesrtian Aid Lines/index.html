Pedestrian Aid Lines
A cooperative road-safety game that translates pedestrian vulnerability into communication, navigation, and map-based decision-making.

Desktop recommended Overview

    Pedestrian Aid Lines is a cooperative road-safety game designed to introduce young players to the needs of vulnerable pedestrians in urban traffic environments.

    The game positions one player as a phone operator for the Pedestrian Aid Line, while another player navigates the city as a pedestrian seeking help. Through conversation, map reading, and destination guidance, players must work together to solve mobility challenges and guide pedestrians safely through the city.

    Rather than presenting road safety as a set of instructions, the project turns it into a shared act of care. The game asks players to listen, interpret, guide, and make decisions under spatial constraints.  


Research Question     How can a cooperative game help young players understand pedestrian safety, accessibility, and the diverse mobility needs of people in urban environments?  

Context     Road safety is often taught through warnings, rules, or fear-based messages. This project takes a different approach by using cooperative play to make safety relational. Instead of asking players only to memorize traffic rules, the game asks them to understand another person’s situation and respond through guidance.

    The project is set in a fictional sustainable city where infrastructure, public services, and safe mobility are treated as shared civic responsibilities. Players encounter pedestrians with different needs and must use the city map, available facilities, and communication cues to help them reach appropriate destinations.  


Game System     The game is built around three connected systems:

1. Communication
    The phone operator receives requests from pedestrians and must understand their needs through dialogue and situation-based cues.

2. Navigation
    Players use a city map to identify suitable destinations and guide pedestrians through the safest possible route.

3. Accessibility Awareness
    Each mission introduces a different mobility challenge, encouraging players to consider how infrastructure affects pedestrians who may be vulnerable, disabled, rushed, confused, or in need of assistance.  


Design Approach     My role focused on translating the educational goal into a visual and spatial experience that felt approachable for children while still carrying civic meaning.

    As game designer and art director, I worked on the world concept, visual tone, character direction, environment logic, and the relationship between map-based gameplay and road-safety learning. The city was designed to feel friendly and playful, but also structured enough to support wayfinding, decision-making, and repeated learning.

    The visual language avoids overly realistic danger. Instead, it uses color, simplified forms, and character-based scenarios to make road safety understandable through empathy and action.

Outcome     The final outcome is a playable cooperative game in which players learn about road safety through shared responsibility rather than passive instruction.

    By placing one player in the role of an operator and another in the role of a pedestrian, the game transforms safety awareness into a process of listening, interpreting, and guiding. The map becomes both a gameplay device and an educational tool, helping players understand how mobility is shaped by infrastructure, accessibility, and decision-making.  





Reflection     Pedestrian Aid Lines helped me understand game design as a system for civic empathy. In this project, play is not used only for entertainment, but as a way to rehearse attention, responsibility, and care for others in public space.

    The project also clarified my interest in design as a functional form of communication. By combining character-based storytelling, map logic, and cooperative decision-making, the game turns an abstract social issue into a set of actions that players can understand through experience.
