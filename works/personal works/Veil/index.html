Veil
A psychological horror game exploring morality, justice, and truth through decision-based gameplay.
Index




Type
      Psychological horror game, decision-based game

Year
      2025

Topic
      Human moral concept

Theme
      Is human inherently good or evil?

Role
      Director, conceptual artist, 3D modeller, sound designer, look developer

Tools / Methods
      Unreal Engine, PSX retro post-processing, 3D modelling, texture painting, dialogue system, note-reading system, interaction system

View Process Book Overview

    Veil is a psychological horror game framed as a social experiment. The project challenges players to confront questions of morality, justice, and truth through decision-based gameplay.

    The player takes the role of Justine, a young forensic doctor searching for the truth behind his mother’s unsolved murder. After discovering a hidden passage beneath her office, he descends into a secret underground facility that first appears to be a research lab studying criminal psychology.

    As the player moves deeper into the facility, the laboratory reveals imprisoned humanoid creatures: test subjects whose bodies have become grotesque manifestations of their past crimes. The project asks whether human morality is innate, shaped by circumstance, or revealed through choice.  








Research Question     Is human nature inherently good or evil, or are moral qualities shaped by circumstance?  

Topic Research     The project began from the question of whether people are born good, born evil, or shaped by the situations they experience. The research looked at human behavior, morality, obedience, authority, and the potential for cruelty when people are placed under pressure or given power without clear consequences.

    References included the Stanford Prison Experiment, Milgram’s Experiment, and Marina Abramović’s Rhythm 0, each used as a way to think about how ordinary people may act when power, authority, or consequence is altered.  


Narrative     The player controls Justine, a forensic doctor in his late twenties. He is tired, gloomy, and still haunted by his mother’s mysterious death. Her case has been closed for years, but Justine becomes a forensic doctor in an attempt to find more clues and reopen the truth behind it.

    While going through his mother’s old office, Justine discovers a secret elevator hidden behind a shelf. The elevator leads him into a dark underground facility with negative floor numbers. Inside, he encounters The Jury, an AI hologram shaped like an eye, and begins moving through laboratories, document rooms, holding cells, and experiment spaces.  


Characters Justine
    The main character. A forensic doctor in his late twenties, trying to uncover the truth behind his mother’s death while facing challenges that test his morality.

The Jury
    An AI hologram shaped like an eye. The Jury appears at the beginning of the game as a tutorial guide, then continues to appear as a “devil on the shoulder” figure, watching the player throughout the facility.

Jeff Hungar / The Cannibal Killer
    The first NPC encountered in the laboratory. He is a serial killer who consumed his victims for closure and comfort. The player meets him trapped on a surgical bed after being tortured by a previous visitor.

The 3-human Monster
    A monstrous NPC made from three convicted sex offenders who tortured and killed many women. They are experimented on and transformed into a monster that reflects their crimes.

The Melted-face Woman
    An NPC held captive for assault after a failed revenge attempt. Her face was melted by acid thrown by her victim.  


Game Mechanics     The core loop begins with the introduction to the story, followed by Justine entering a secret door inside his mother’s workplace. The player then explores the first interactive space, collects key items, reads files, interacts with NPCs, and unlocks new areas.

    In the first lab, the player encounters Jeff Hungar tied to a surgical bed. After interacting with him, The Jury presents two options: release him or torture him.

    If the player releases Jeff, he chases the player and the game ends. If the player tortures him, Justine can shock him to death. The player may also collect objects that unlock new torture methods. After Jeff dies, The Jury guides Justine to the next area.  


Visual Direction     The visual direction combines psychological horror, grotesque body imagery, supernatural atmosphere, and retro game aesthetics. The moodboard references confined and unsettling environments, low-poly retro horror games, distorted mixed-media horror imagery, corpse paintings, grotesque sculpture, and depictions of violence and hell.

    The humanoid test subjects were designed to remain partially human while exaggerating specific body parts that suggest their manifestations, obsessions, or crimes. This approach was used to create an emotional connection between the player and the creatures, since their bodies remain recognizable.  


Set Design     The setting was designed to feel creepy and unsettling, drawing from old mental hospitals, prison cells, investigation rooms, and police station spaces. The main areas include holding cells, lab scenes, observation/archive rooms, and torture scenes.

    The environment uses practical lights and repetitive, plain furniture to build a mysterious atmosphere. The team modelled furniture for the lab, prison cells, office, document room, reception area, and elevator. Additional decorative objects such as the Human Spider sculpture, Human Pool, Arms Wing, and hell-themed paintings were added to make the laboratory resonate with the game’s concept.  


3D Character / Jeff Hungar     Jeff Hungar was developed in two models: one before being tortured and cut up, and another for the extra ending after the player chooses to torture him.

    His first model shows him trapped on a surgical bed, bruised and wounded after being tortured by the previous visitor. The second model is more heavily morphed, with heads from his victims pushing out of his stomach. The textures include bruises, blood, veins, and skinned-head details.

    To achieve the low-resolution pixelated effect, the team exported textures at 256x256 pixels. Jeff was rigged in Blender with Rigify, processed with GameRigTools, and exported to Unreal as a skeletal mesh with animations including attack, idle, run, and tied states.  


UI     The UI was developed to match the overall PSX retro game look. The team studied retro UI references from Resident Evil 1, Mouthwashing, and Final Fantasy VII to inform the menu, dialogue, inventory, and information graphics.

    The game also includes a death scene. When the player loses, an animated cutscene appears with a pixelated effect applied over blood-splatter imagery, creating a horrific end-state screen.

    Because many NPCs have backstories, the game includes in-game documents such as criminal records, evidence lists, mugshots, victim photos, and crime-scene remains for the player to read.  


Technical Integration     The game was built in Unreal Engine. The team used primitive shapes and basic lighting to block out the level, managing the project with data layers for greyboxing, lighting, and detailed geometry.

    To achieve the PSX retro look, the camera uses post-processing effects such as pixelation, dithering, jittering, and outlines. The first-person character was simplified to a camera and collision system.

    The interaction system supports doors, notes, computers, dialogue options, and note-reading UI. Jeff’s interaction includes two buttons: torture and release. The torture option plays the shock animation, lightning effect, scream sound, and reduces his HP. The release option switches Jeff into a run animation and allows him to chase and attack the player.

    The Jury is implemented as a static mesh with procedural floating and look-at-player animation. Overlay materials were added to interactable objects, and the lightning effect on Jeff was created with a procedural material.  


Collaborators
  • Nguyen Thu Trang: Director, conceptual artist, 3D modeller, sound designer, look developer
  • Nguyen Thai Xuan Bach: 2D artist and illustrator, character rigging and animation, texture painter, character designer
  • Huynh Trung Hieu: Code developer, sound designer, character rigging and animation


Outcome     The final outcome is a psychological horror game prototype that turns the question of human morality into a decision-based experience. Through Justine’s descent into the underground facility, the player encounters criminal test subjects, reads documents, makes choices, and faces the consequences of release or torture.

    The project combines narrative, horror atmosphere, character design, retro visual treatment, and interactive mechanics to create a game space where morality becomes something the player must act upon.
