Veil
A psychological horror game exploring morality, justice, and truth through decision-based gameplay.
Index


Gameplay atmosphere and visual direction for Veil.


Type
      Psychological horror game, decision-based game

Year
      2025

Topic
      Human moral concept

Theme
      Is human inherently good or evil?

Role
      Director, conceptual artist, 3D modeller, sound designer, look developer

Tools / Methods
      Unreal Engine, PSX retro post-processing, 3D modelling, texture painting, dialogue system, note-reading system, interaction system

Process Book below Overview

    Veil began with a question our team could not answer easily: are people inherently good or evil, or do circumstances decide what they become? We turned that question into a psychological horror game where the player cannot stay outside the problem. Every decision asks them to judge another person and live with the result.  








Research Question     Is human nature inherently good or evil, or are moral qualities shaped by circumstance?  

Topic Research     We studied the Stanford Prison Experiment, Milgram’s Experiment, and Marina Abramovic’s Rhythm 0. What connected these references was how quickly ordinary people could become cruel when authority encouraged them or when consequences disappeared. That tension became the foundation of the game.  


Narrative     The player becomes Justine, a young forensic doctor still haunted by his mother’s unsolved murder. While packing her old office, he discovers a secret elevator leading to negative floors beneath the building. There he meets The Jury, an eye-shaped AI that first behaves like a guide and gradually becomes the devil on his shoulder.

    The facility holds criminal test subjects whose bodies have been transformed to reflect their crimes. One of them may also be connected to the truth about Justine’s mother.  


Characters Justine — A forensic doctor investigating his mother’s death while his morality is tested.

The Jury — An eye-shaped AI that shifts from tutorial guide to watchful provocateur.

Jeff Hungar — A cannibalistic killer found restrained after an earlier visitor’s torture.

The 3-human Monster — Three convicted offenders fused into a body that reflects their crimes.

The Melted-face Woman — A captive whose failed revenge left her disfigured by acid.  


Game Mechanics     Players explore the facility, read criminal records, collect evidence, and question NPCs before making decisions. The first subject, Jeff Hungar, begs to be released from a surgical bed. The player can free him and risk being attacked, or torture him under The Jury’s instruction. We wanted the choice to feel uncomfortable rather than obviously right or wrong.  


Visual Direction     We were drawn to the claustrophobic spaces of Iron Lung, the low-poly character language of Mouthwashing, and the mixed-media nightmares of Omori. We combined those references with Goya, Fuyuko Matsui, Choi Xooang, and Ronit Baranga to create a PSX-style world that feels supernatural, institutional, and physically uncomfortable.

    The test subjects remain recognizably human because we wanted players to see a body like their own before seeing the monster.  


Set Design     Old mental hospitals, prison cells, morgues, and investigation rooms shaped the facility. We used repetitive furniture and practical lighting to make it feel controlled and believable, then filled it with stranger objects - a Human Spider sculpture, a Human Pool, an Arms Wing, and paintings about hell - so the space slowly stops feeling rational.  


3D Character / Jeff Hungar     Jeff was the first NPC we developed. I worked on two versions: the bruised body found on the surgical bed and a more heavily transformed version where the heads of his victims push through his stomach. When our high-resolution textures looked too clean, we exported them at 256×256 pixels, repainted key details, and used the limitation to strengthen the retro look.  


UI     References from Resident Evil, Mouthwashing, and Final Fantasy VII shaped the PSX-style menus, dialogue, inventory, and death screen. Criminal records, mugshots, and evidence files carry the NPC backstories.  


Technical Integration     We built the prototype in Unreal Engine, beginning with a simple greybox and separating lighting and detailed geometry into data layers. The PSX look comes from pixelation, dithering, jitter, outlines, and low-resolution textures. On top of that, we developed the dialogue, note-reading, object interaction, NPC pursuit, procedural electricity, and The Jury’s floating eye animation.  


Collaborators
  • Nguyen Thu Trang (yuuwouldnever): Director, conceptual artist, 3D modeller, sound designer, look developer
  • Nguyen Thai Xuan Bach: 2D artist and illustrator, character rigging and animation, texture painter, character designer
  • Huynh Trung Hieu: Code developer, sound designer, character rigging and animation


Outcome     The final prototype turns a philosophical question into something the player has to perform with their own hands. For me, the most important outcome was learning how narrative, character design, environment, sound, and interaction can work together to make a moral decision feel personal.
