Kieu
A speculative 3D character design exploring Vietnamese diaspora identity, historical dress, and contemporary fashion remix.
Index


Final render of the character.


Type
      3D character design, speculative fashion, cultural remix

Year
      2023

Role
      Concept, character design, styling, 3D art

Methods
      Character research, historical reference, fashion remix, 3D modeling and rendering     Overview

    Kieu is a speculative 3D character design project centered on Hoa Kieu, a Vietnamese diaspora character living in the United States who unexpectedly travels back to the Nguyen Dynasty after trying on her grandmother’s old “ao yem”.

    The project uses time travel as a fictional device to explore cultural dislocation, inherited identity, and the tension between tradition and self-styling. Rather than presenting Vietnamese heritage as something fixed or untouchable, the character treats it as a living material: something that can be misread, reassembled, worn, exaggerated, and claimed again.

    Through 3D character design, Kieu examines how fashion can become a site where cultural memory, diaspora attitude, and contemporary image-making collide.  


Research Question     How can a fictional character use fashion remix to express the unstable relationship between inherited culture, diaspora identity, and contemporary self-image?  

Context     The project began with an interest in Vietnamese cultural identity as something that is often experienced indirectly: through family stories, inherited garments, online images, pop culture, and partial knowledge.

    Hoa Kieu is not designed as a historically accurate figure. She is a speculative character who embodies contradiction. She is proud of being Vietnamese, but her relationship to Vietnamese culture is fragmented, playful, performative, and sometimes awkward. This tension becomes the core of the project.

    By placing a contemporary diaspora character inside a historical setting, Kieu creates a fictional collision between past and present. The result is not a reconstruction of tradition, but a character study about how young people negotiate heritage through style, humor, exaggeration, and self-invention.  


Character Concept     Hoa Kieu is a “temporal criminal”: a character who breaks the rules of historical dress by bringing contemporary diaspora fashion into a dynastic setting.

    Her styling combines references to Vietnamese traditional garments, especially the áo yếm, with elements associated with contemporary youth fashion, streetwear, and online identity performance. The character is intentionally excessive. She is not trying to blend into the past; she enters it as disruption.

    Through this disruption, the project asks whether cultural belonging has to be expressed through correctness, or whether it can also emerge through remix, affection, and imperfect return.  


Design Approach     The character was developed through three design directions:

1. Historical Reference
    Vietnamese garment references were used as a foundation for silhouette, layering, and cultural context, while avoiding a purely documentary approach.

2. Contemporary Styling
    Streetwear, diaspora pop culture, and internet-driven fashion cues were introduced to create tension between inherited tradition and present-day self-image.

3. Speculative Characterization
    The final character does not represent a single historical period or a stable identity. Instead, she becomes a visual contradiction: nostalgic and artificial, proud and uncertain, familiar and out of place.  


Outcome     The final outcome is a 3D character that uses fashion, posture, material, and styling to visualize a hybrid cultural identity. Hoa Kieu becomes both a fictional persona and a design experiment: a way to test how heritage can be translated through contemporary visual language without becoming either costume or cliché.

    The work positions character design as more than visual styling. It becomes a method for staging cultural tension, humor, and self-recognition.  


Reflection     Kieu helped me understand character design as a space for negotiating identity. Through this project, I became interested in how visual design can hold contradiction without resolving it.

    The character is not meant to be a perfect cultural ambassador. She is messy, performative, excessive, and emotionally sincere. For me, that imperfection is important. It reflects how cultural identity is often lived: not as a complete archive, but as a process of remembering, misunderstanding, inheriting, styling, and returning.
