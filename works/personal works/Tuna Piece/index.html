Tuna Piece
A 2D motion graphic interpreting Yoko Ono’s “Tunafish Sandwich Piece” through transience, wabi-sabi, and the quiet return from grandeur to everyday life.
Index




Type
      2D motion graphic, illustration, experimental animation

Year
      2025

Reference
      “Tunafish Sandwich Piece” by Yoko Ono, 1964

Role
      Concept, 2D illustration, motion design, character rigging, compositing

Tools / Methods
      Photoshop, Illustrator, After Effects, DUIK rigging, Risograph color palette, watercolor texture, canvas paper texture, noise, Turbulent Displacement

Process Book below Overview

    Tuna Piece is my 2D motion interpretation of Yoko Ono’s 1964 instruction work, “Tunafish Sandwich Piece.” What interested me was its sudden shift from an impossible image - one thousand suns filling the sky - to the very ordinary act of making and eating a tuna sandwich.

    I used that contrast to move between two emotional spaces: a short moment of grandeur and the quietness of everyday life.  





Design Question     How can a short instruction piece be translated into a visual movement from extraordinary grandeur to mundane simplicity?  

Concept     The poem gives the suns only one hour before they gradually melt into the sky. I read this as a reminder that even the most magnificent experiences are temporary. The tuna sandwich brings us back to ordinary life, where small actions still matter.

    This feeling connected with wabi-sabi: accepting the imperfect, impermanent, and incomplete beauty of daily life.  


Visual References     I first looked at Ukiyo-e paintings because of the way they describe daily life with minimal brushwork and generous negative space. Seiichi Hayashi helped me find a more restrained illustration style, while Oceane Muller inspired the dreamlike gradients, textures, and brushstrokes.  


Storyboard     The animation begins with a girl lying at home and doing nothing. When she closes her eyes, the room becomes a performance stage filled with lights, then opens into a flower field at the peak of her emotion. A burning flare finally brings her back to the same quiet room.  


Art Direction     After several visual tests, I chose a Risograph-inspired palette because it felt soft, printed, and imperfect. I layered watercolor, canvas paper, noise, and Turbulent Displacement in After Effects so the digital animation would keep a handmade surface.  


Motion Process     I planned the dance with storyboard frames and movement references from Black Swan. I drew and separated the character in Illustrator, rigged it with DUIK, then animated and composited the scenes in After Effects.  


Technical Development     Some Illustrator strokes and textures did not survive the transfer into After Effects, so I had to rebuild them and reorganize the converted shape layers. This took time, but it taught me much more about shape paths, 3D camera movement, perspective, and keyframing.  


Outcome     The final animation moves from a quiet room into an imagined performance and back again. I did not want grandeur and ordinary life to feel like opposites; I wanted the extraordinary moment to gently dissolve back into the daily one.  


Reflection     Tuna Piece helped me understand how motion can translate an abstract text without illustrating every sentence literally. Its emotional center is not the largest scene, but the return to something simple: a quiet room, a body at rest, and the acceptance that beautiful moments pass.
