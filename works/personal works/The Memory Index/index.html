The Memory Index
A web-based reflective tool translating personal digital memories into a collective ASCII artwork on traditional student notebook and Do paper.

Featured in Thanh Nien News The Insight

    Born from a personal period of emotional detachment, The Memory Index is a p5.js interactive tool designed to help people slow down and reconnect with their memories. Rather than forcing emotional resolution, it functions as an experimental reflective interface for self-observation.

    Drawing visual cues from 2000s-2010s Vietnamese pop culture - student notebooks, purple ink, administrative study outlines, and the early internet era of Zingme - the project fuses local Vietnamese nostalgia with complex net.art aesthetics. It transforms subjective feelings into a readable, computational visual system.  


How It Works     After evolving through multiple prototypes, from complex 3D character projections to paper fortune-tellers, the final system prioritizes accessible reflection through four steps:

1. Write
    Users input journaling reflections to anchor their feelings in words.

2. Process
    Personal photos are algorithmically converted into computational ASCII textures with a nostalgic, marker-like threshold effect.

3. Sketch
    Replacing freehand mouse drawing, a custom ASCII painting tool lets users compose images by placing characters into a grid - making the act of drawing as deliberate and relaxing as playing with LEGO.

4. Manifest
    Individual digital diagrams are printed on physical student notebook paper or Do paper and physically sewn together to form a large, collective exhibition artwork.  





Process & Technical Development     The project bridges computational code with tactile materiality. Built entirely in p5.js, the tool underwent rigorous UI/UX iterations. I intentionally shifted away from a hyper-complex Mac HyperCard aesthetic toward a layout mimicking Vietnamese administrative documents, optimized with CRT-style monospaced fonts for analog exhibition displays.

    Technical friction was actively solved during user testing: I implemented local storage so users wouldn't lose their progress upon refreshing, and capped the frame rate to 24 FPS to prevent browser crashes during heavy image processing. A crucial phase involved print testing to find the right tactile fidelity, comparing student grid paper with traditional Vietnamese Do paper for the final output.  



Project showcased at IFFTI 2026 Conference.


Exhibition & Outcome     The result is a system for emotional translation. Instead of isolated, customized outputs for each user, the project shifted toward a large collective artwork. By taking digital inputs, printing them on raw Do paper, and stitching them together with embroidery thread - a familiar childhood gesture - the project proves that computational design can facilitate deeply personal, collective human healing.

    Recognition: The project was officially showcased at IFFTI 2026, marking the first time a Vietnamese educational institution hosted the 28th Annual Conference of the International Federation of Fashion Institutes of Technology. The work and its exhibition were featured in national media, including Thanh Nien News.
