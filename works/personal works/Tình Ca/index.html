Tình Ca (Love Song)
A typographic piece reconstructing Vietnamese vernacular letterforms through archival publication, music.
Index




Type
      Typographic piece, lettering reconstruction, Vietnamese vernacular typography

Year
      2024

References
      Luc Van Tien old book cover
      “Tình Ca” by Pham Duy

Methods
      Archival visual research, hand redrawing, vector reconstruction, typographic system development

View Poster     Overview

    Tình Ca is a crafted typographic piece developed from Vietnamese archival and vernacular visual references. The project began from a brief to study old Vietnamese publications, street billboards, and printed lettering, then transform those references into an original typographic composition.

    I selected a lettering sample from an old cover of Luc Van Tien and used it as the foundation for the project. The letterforms carried a literary and tale-like quality, while also recalling the script-like forms often seen in Vietnamese printed culture.

    The project takes its emotional register from Pham Duy’s “Tình Ca.” Rather than illustrating the song directly, the piece uses its sense of tenderness, memory, and cultural affection as a way to guide the rhythm of the lettering.  




Research Question     How can archival Vietnamese lettering be reconstructed into a contemporary typographic piece while preserving its literary, emotional, and vernacular qualities?  

Context     The project started from my interest in Vietnamese literary history and everyday printed culture. Old publications and street lettering often contain forms that are expressive but informal, refined but unstable. These qualities became the starting point for the work.

    The source lettering from Lục Vân Tiên suggested a narrative tone: decorative, literary, and connected to oral storytelling. At the same time, the song “Tình Ca” introduced another layer of feeling, allowing the typography to move beyond historical reference and become a visual expression of affection, memory, and cultural familiarity.  


Method     The process developed through three stages:

1. Source Study
    I began by observing the original letterforms from the Luc Van Tien cover, focusing on their contrast, curves, rhythm, and ornamental qualities.

2. Redrawing
    The letters were redrawn by hand and then translated into vector form. This stage allowed me to understand the internal logic of the forms rather than only copying their surface appearance.

3. System Development
    After redrawing, I adjusted the letterforms into a more consistent guide system. The goal was not to clean the source completely, but to balance its irregular charm with a functional typographic structure.  


Typographic Approach     The piece treats typography as both image and voice. Instead of designing neutral letterforms, I wanted the type to carry a sense of song, memory, and old printed matter.

    The final composition keeps the softness and expressive rhythm of the source lettering, while refining its proportions into a clearer system. This tension between preservation and improvisation became the main design method of the project.  


Outcome     The final outcome is a typographic composition that translates Vietnamese archival lettering into a contemporary visual piece. It does not attempt to reproduce the original cover literally. Instead, it uses the source as a structure for reinterpreting cultural memory through form, rhythm, and craft.

    By connecting the literary reference of Luc Van Tien with the emotional atmosphere of “Tình Ca,” the project creates a piece of typography that feels both historical and personal.  


Reflection     Tình Ca helped me understand lettering as a way of studying cultural memory. Through the process of redrawing, I was not only reconstructing forms, but also learning how visual details can carry emotion, history, and local identity.

    The project clarified my interest in Vietnamese vernacular design: not as nostalgia alone, but as a living visual language that can be reworked, systematized, and brought into contemporary practice.
