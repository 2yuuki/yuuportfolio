“Imagine a future in which humans evolve to survive in a world lacking oxygen.”

The idea began with a question: if humans have always adapted in order to survive, what might we become in a future without enough oxygen? The Climate Clock made that question feel urgent because its countdown shows that adaptation is not the same as having unlimited time.

The story synopsis:
           In a toxic, plantless city, a man tries to save Earth by merging with a seed. Roots grow through his body, but his apparent success becomes a fatal illusion: he is burying himself alive as the Climate Clock reaches zero.

We made the character as a physical stop-motion puppet and built the city in CGI so we could create a larger world around his scale. The process was full of practical problems: the armature had to be remade several times, the green-screen lighting had to match the digital environment, and the cardboard textures needed to feel consistent across both physical and 3D elements.

We modelled recognizable details from Sai Gon - store signs, flags, public speakers, tangled power lines, dry branches, and piles of rubbish - so the apocalypse would still feel close to home. The final film combines stop motion, Blender renders, keying, rotoscoping, matte painting, colour grading, and a found-footage interface.

I wanted the work to leave a simple message: we should act before the clock reaches zero, rather than waiting to see how humans might survive afterward.
