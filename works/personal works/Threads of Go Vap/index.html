Threads of Go Vap
An interactive typographic poster translating the spatial texture of Gò Vấp Market into scan-based imagery, motion, and responsive type.
Index




Type
      Interactive poster, typography, motion, spatial documentation

Year
      2024

Role
      Concept, visual research, 3D scanning, typographic system, interaction and motion design

Tools / Methods
      3D scan, TouchDesigner, typographic experimentation, neighborhood observation

View Process Book Overview

    Threads of Go Vap is an interactive typographic poster developed from the visual and spatial identity of Gò Vấp Market, a place closely tied to my daily life and childhood memory.

    The project began as an attempt to re-observe a familiar environment that had become almost invisible through repetition. By collecting material from the market through spatial documentation and 3D scanning, I translated its density, movement, and everyday texture into a typographic system activated through motion and interaction.

    Rather than representing the market as a static location, the poster treats it as a living surface: crowded, layered, unstable, and constantly in motion.  


Research Question     How can interactive typography translate the sensory and spatial identity of a familiar neighborhood into a visual experience?  

Context     I have lived in Gò Vấp since I was born. For a long time, the market existed as a constant background to my routines: ordinary, familiar, and easy to overlook. Returning to it through this project allowed me to see the place not as a backdrop, but as a visual system shaped by movement, signage, material traces, street rhythm, and accumulated memory.

    The work is positioned between personal observation and urban documentation. It uses the market as a site to examine how everyday environments carry identity through small visual details: temporary structures, textures, informal arrangements, handwritten signs, traffic, noise, repetition, and human presence.  


Method     The project developed through three connected methods:

1. Observation
    I revisited Go Vap Market as both a resident and a designer, paying attention to forms that are usually ignored because of their familiarity.

2. Spatial Capture
    I used 3D scanning to collect fragments of the market environment. These scans became raw visual material, carrying the imperfections, density, and distortion of the site.

3. Typographic Translation
    The scanned material was combined with interactive typography in TouchDesigner, allowing type to behave less like fixed text and more like a moving surface shaped by the energy of the market.  











PERFORMANCE




System     The visual system is built from the idea of threads: lines, routes, fragments, signs, and movements that overlap within the market space.

    Typography is used not only to label the site, but to behave like part of it. The letters stretch, distort, accumulate, and move, echoing the way market information appears in real life: layered across signs, voices, objects, stalls, and pathways.

    By combining scanned material with interactive type, the project turns the poster into a responsive surface rather than a fixed composition.  


Outcome     The final outcome is an interactive poster that reinterprets Gò Vấp Market through typography, motion, and spatial texture. It translates a familiar neighborhood site into a visual experience that can be read, watched, and felt through movement.

    The project also functions as a personal act of re-seeing. By documenting and transforming the market, I was able to reconnect with a place that had shaped my everyday life but had become visually overlooked.  


Reflection     Threads of Go Vap helped me understand typography as a tool for spatial and emotional translation. Type does not only communicate language; it can also carry atmosphere, memory, movement, and place.

    Through this project, I became interested in how design can make familiar environments visible again. The work proposes that a neighborhood can be read not only through maps or photographs, but through the small visual systems that shape daily life.
